01 / WHAT MAKES ME TICK

What makes
me tick.

I've never been very good at staying in one lane.

I've worked on everything from naval engineering contract support to clinical asset informatics to systems used to plan and operate the electric grid, with a few startup adventures along the way.

I've built technology functions from scratch, helped reinvigorate stalled transformations, and led teams across Software Quality Assurance, DevOps, Service Delivery, and Product Management. I've also been employee number one, in the trenches helping launch a startup and venture studio.

I especially enjoy the early days of figuring something out. Asking lots of questions. Getting to know people who understand things I don't. Listening. And helping smart people bring their best selves and ideas to the table.

The common thread? Possibility.

I love imagining what could exist that doesn't yet, or what could be so much better than it is today. But what really gets me going is bringing people together around that possibility, helping create the conditions for them to do their best work, and seeing what we can make happen together.

02 / A FEW THINGS I'VE HELPED MAKE HAPPEN

A few things
I’ve been part of.

Three chapters, and a few things I learned along the way.

01TRIMEDX · HEALTHCARE TECHNOLOGY2013—2023
◎

Good teams don’t happen by accident.

When I was asked to build a Software Quality Assurance function at TRIMEDX, I had an opportunity to do something I'd never done before: build a team from scratch.

I got pretty interested in what makes software teams effective. I read Accelerate, dug into the research behind the DORA State of DevOps reports, and started questioning some of the conventional wisdom about how development, testing, and operations should work together.

One thing became increasingly clear to me: quality wasn't something you could inspect into a product at the end. And high-performing teams weren't built by getting everyone to work harder within their own little silos.

So we worked to bring testing closer to development, build automation into the way teams delivered software, and make quality something everyone owned.

But the part that stuck with me wasn't really about testing.

It was discovering how much better work could be when people trusted each other, had room to make decisions, and actually enjoyed solving problems together.

That experience helped shape a conviction I've carried ever since: help teams have fun building cool stuff, together.

02DAST · VENTURE STUDIO2023—2025
✳

Betting on myself.

Leaving TRIMEDX after ten years wasn't an easy decision.

I had a great job, people I loved working with, and plenty of opportunities to keep growing. And with a family depending on one income, walking away from that kind of stability wasn't something I took lightly.

But I had an itch I couldn't quite shake.

I'd spent much of my career helping other people and teams succeed. I was good at it, and I genuinely enjoyed it. But I wanted to stretch myself. To step outside what I knew, take a chance on something new, and find out what I could do.

So I took the leap and helped launch a venture studio.

I was employee number one, working alongside the founder to turn an ambitious idea into an actual business. There was no playbook. One day I might be shaping a product, the next talking with potential customers, hiring a team, or figuring out something neither of us had ever done before.

I loved it.

We explored five ventures. Three ultimately didn't make it past our go/no-go decisions. Two went on to raise meaningful investment capital and land national contracts.

It was a wild couple of years. Some ideas worked. Others didn't. I learned a ton, occasionally the hard way. And I came away with an even bigger appetite for the kind of work where the path forward isn't obvious and there's something new to build.

03MISO · ELECTRIC GRID2025—2026
↗

The tip of the spear.

During my ten years at TRIMEDX, I had a front-row seat to a major enterprise digital transformation. I'd seen what worked, what didn't, and how hard it can be to change the way a large organization builds technology.

When MISO came calling, they were looking for someone who had been there, done that. Someone who could bring an outside perspective to their own product transformation.

That sounded like a lot of fun.

I'd never held a formal Product Management title before. But I'd worked alongside product teams for years, led product work in the venture studio, and spent much of my career helping teams rethink how they worked.

I wanted to be the tip of the spear. Not just observe another transformation, but help lead one.

And I quickly discovered that transforming how an organization builds technology is a lot more complicated than introducing new roles, processes, or frameworks. It's about helping people see things differently, challenging assumptions that have become second nature, and figuring out how to make meaningful change in an organization that still has a business to run.

One of the most exciting things I worked on was helping our leadership team rethink the product operating model using ideas from Team Topologies. We brought leaders together to explore different ways of organizing teams, reducing complexity, and giving people more room to focus on the problems they were actually there to solve.

At TRIMEDX, I'd helped bring a bold vision to life. At MISO, I got to help shape the vision itself.
